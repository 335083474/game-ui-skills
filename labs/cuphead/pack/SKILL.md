# 1930s rubber-hose cartoon game UI

This package provides a visual grammar, reusable fragments, and synthetic interaction contracts for original game interfaces. Its names and values are fictional examples.

## Read and inspect

- Read [visual-rules.md](references/visual-rules.md), [tokens.json](references/tokens.json), and the requested pattern in [screen-patterns.md](references/screen-patterns.md).
- Inspect the original generated plate at [cuphead-art.png](assets/illustrations/cuphead-art.png). Use a crop as an art cue; do not stretch it across a screen.
- Build from [editable component specimens](assets/components/index.html) and [theme.css](assets/components/theme.css).
- Match synthetic data and observable behavior to [demo-state.json](references/demo-state.json).
- Confirm edition and source limits in [sources.md](references/sources.md).

## Direction rules

Bold, elastic, theatrical, and print-inspired. Use thick black keylines, cream stock, limited spot reds and greens, scalloped bursts, two-tone print registration. Favor clear silhouettes and readable action labels. The art may carry atmosphere, while UI state stays in HTML text and accessible semantics. a modern card dashboard, exact cup-headed or kettle characters, copied bosses, excessive fake film scratches, low-contrast yellow text.

Choose the pattern that matches the request: equipment-cards, shop-counter, or level-results. Compose each requested surface for its actual player task; this board is a specimen sheet, never a full application. Keep scene space visible for active play and place secondary reference material behind an intentional open/close control.

Selection and preview must be reversible until explicit confirmation. Explain cost, target, and invalid use in words. Make pointer, keyboard, and assistive-technology feedback equivalent. Honor reduced motion and maintain readable contrast over painted scenes.
