# Screen patterns

Use one original class, one investigation, three exhibits, and one witness statement across all requested screens. These contracts describe UI grammar and state, not finished screen layouts.

## Student handbook

Use a compact ring index or numbered tab strip to move among student file, map, and investigation notes. The current section is marked with text, number, and pink / black shape. The selected student page carries one portrait, name, class role, and short invented notes. Keep navigation separate from data so switching pages does not erase a selected clue. Do not use a full-screen rotating wheel as the only input; provide a linear keyboard path and direct labels.

Synthetic example: student “Nari Vale,” archivist assistant; page 02 “Investigation.” All biography and layout content are invented.

## Evidence locker

Show evidence as a compact numbered sequence of selectable shells, each with a short name and source. Selecting an item opens a detail strip with date / location and one to three known facts. Keep unknown or disputed details labeled. A “link to statement” action is separate from selection, and a successful link explains which claim it supports. Returning from a trial must restore the prior selected evidence and scroll position. Avoid a grid of colorful generic inventory cards.

Synthetic example: EP-03 “Hall Clock Photo,” taken in the east corridor at 22:14. It shows a stopped hand but does not prove when the clock stopped.

## Trial debate

Show one active testimony fragment at a time, with a small sequence / timer indicator and an evidence-token row. The player first selects an evidence token, then a specific claim, then explicitly submits the match. A correct result identifies the evidence fact that conflicts and advances one step. A miss explains the mismatch and returns to selection without consuming evidence. Back clears the pending token, never the shared evidence list. Keep controls direct and legible; any fast movement must be optional and have a pause / reduced-motion path.

Synthetic example: claim “The clock read 22:00 after the lights failed.” EP-03 shows the hands stopped at 22:14, which is a possible contradiction but not proof of timing. The UI reports that nuance instead of asserting guilt.
