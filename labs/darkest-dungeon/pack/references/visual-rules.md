# Visual rules — gothic ink direction

The official store description names a hand-drawn gothic crowquill art style and psychological stress as a core part of adventuring. This package translates those signals into an original expedition ledger; it does not reproduce exact game interface construction.

## Material and line

- Treat the surface like a soot-dark ledger: coal fields, stained parchment, uneven rules, and thin brass edges.
- Draw dry, broken contours around portraits and objects. Balance dense silhouettes with small etched scratches; avoid making every edge equally thick.
- Keep strong black shapes readable at thumbnail size. Use original figures and supply marks instead of emoji or generic outline icons.
- Reserve vermilion for stress, danger, or a decisive warning. Brass and smoke-gray may mark rank and rarity.

## Type and hierarchy

- Use locally installed serif system fonts for names and headings; a monospace system face can mark terse quantities and ledger labels.
- Set the hero or choice name first, then one gameplay value, then the smallest contextual label.
- Stress deserves a dedicated red meter or notch. Do not hide it inside a general status card.

## Approved patterns

- **expedition-roster:** compact silhouette, rank, vitality, and stress in one row. Critical stress must be communicated with shape and text as well as color.
- **provision-shop:** keep quantity and cost adjacent to a small original supply illustration; show the limit in a ledger footline.
- **expedition-reward:** present a few distinct heirloom choices, with one etched focus frame and an explicit take or abandon action.

## Play and adaptation

If the design overlays a playable dungeon, collapse it to a small edge cluster and keep the center and lower-middle playfield clear. Put the full roster and reward choices behind a pause or transition state. Keep keyboard focus visible with a brass edge.
