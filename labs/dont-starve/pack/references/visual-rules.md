# Visual rules

## Shape and material

Use warm paper, soot-black linework, and limited root-brown and ember-orange accents. Frames should look hand-cut or scratched: corners can lean, edges can wobble, and small hatch marks can sit outside the silhouette. Keep enough clean paper behind body text to protect legibility.

## Type and survival signals

Use a local book serif for headings and a typewriter or monospace face for counts and day marks. Hunger, health, and sanity need separate labeled marks; never rely on color alone. Give danger a sharp red-orange tick, not a broad colored dashboard.

## Item grammar

Recipes show the object first, then its ingredient count and one short use. Inventory squares are rough paper cutouts with visible stack counts. A survival HUD stays sparse over the world; detailed inventory and crafting belong behind a pause surface.

## Adaptation

Use [screen-patterns.md](screen-patterns.md) for crafting, inventory, and a compact survival HUD. The package targets the 2013 single-player direction and does not cover Don't Starve Together. Art and sample labels are original study material.
