# Visual rules

## Material and hierarchy

Use soot charcoal (#202427), cold iron, chalk-white type, and frozen blue-grey. A muted amber or coal ember marks an urgent state. Avoid bright gold and saturated cyan. Snow texture can appear around the outside edge or on object illustrations, while reading surfaces remain stable and opaque.

Large areas stay quiet. Put the temperature or generator state first, city resources second, and law/technology detail behind a selected surface. Separate modules with steel rules and notched corners rather than luminous outlines or bevel-heavy cards. Use thick horizontal dividers to make the city feel engineered and severe.

## Thermal instrument and warning marks

The temperature gauge is a circular dial with a legible numeric temperature at its center. Show thresholds with named ticks or short words such as SAFE, COLD, and DANGEROUS; never rely on color alone. A small red-orange pointer may identify the current reading. The surrounding panel remains still. Avoid animated heat glow or continuous snow particles.

Warnings use an angular notch or narrow side band and a concise cause. Keep warning copy short. A resource strip uses compact icon, name, count, and trend per resource. Unknown or unavailable values remain unknown rather than silently becoming zero.

## Decree and technology components

A law appears on a vertical ordinance slip: title, short consequence, required signature or cost, and status. A proposed law has a clear preview; signing commits its consequence once. Do not render laws as modern rounded cards or a scrolling social feed.

Technology uses branching nodes connected by thin engineered lines. Completed nodes are chalk-bright; available nodes have a steel outline; blocked nodes show their prerequisite in text. Use a modest cost marker near the node. Avoid a giant all-at-once infographic over the city map.

## Responsive and accessible behavior

On desktop, keep one thermal/instrument cluster and a compact resource rail near the top; put detailed choices in a side drawer or pause surface. On mobile, stack the instrument and resources and make the tree independently scrollable. Focus is a high-contrast double line, not a color-only glow. Respect reduced motion and use small transitions only for a threshold crossing or a committed state change.
