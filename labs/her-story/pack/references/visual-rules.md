# Visual rules

## Archive desk

Use a muted paper-and-plastic palette: warm ivory, tobacco brown, faded olive, and one restrained oxide-red signal. The working surface should feel like a late-analog records terminal with a slightly dusty CRT, not a modern media dashboard. Use narrow separators, stamped index labels, compact fixed-width identifiers, and a visible margin around the main work area.

The included palette and panel treatments are original design choices. Avoid copying game screenshots or reproducing proprietary title marks. A subtle scanline or phosphor texture can sit over a media placeholder, but keep it faint enough that labels remain crisp. Do not animate constant flicker.

## Search and evidence hierarchy

Make the search field the clearest action. Results should show a stable clip ID, interview/session label, match context, and an original short transcript excerpt. Show the match count and an empty-results state. A selected result should update the metadata panel and the note-link affordance.

The video frame is a media slot, not a substitute for footage. When no user-supplied licensed clip is available, use a blank slate or clearly labeled original illustration. Keep `SIMULATED / NO FOOTAGE` visible inside or directly beside the frame. A transport-shaped button may select a mock clip state; its status copy must say that no playback occurred.

## Reusable components

- Database search: compact query, submit affordance, and result count.
- Clip row: monospaced ID, short label, match phrase, and selected state.
- Media slot: frame edge, timecode-like placeholder, and a persistent simulation disclosure.
- Case note: editable title, source clip reference, and short original note text.
- Evidence links: connect a note to a clip ID; do not imply hidden clues or canon facts.

## Responsive and accessible behavior

At desktop widths, search results and the selected-clip panel may sit side by side. At narrow widths, put results before the selected-clip details and keep the query visible. Use semantic forms and buttons, visible focus, text labels in addition to color, and a reduced-motion-safe scanline treatment. Do not use autoplay or automatically start audio/video.
