# Approved screen patterns

The three patterns describe distinct player tasks. The specimen board is a kit of fragments, not a final screen.

## party

Make the active member and each member's role or condition legible before showing secondary detail. Use an irregular orbit or loose clay lineup, not a conventional evenly sized portrait grid. Selection previews a member; a separate “Set active” action commits. Keep the roster keyboard reachable when the board collapses on mobile.

## gesture-battle

Show the acting member, a small set of distinct gesture choices, the target, and the likely outcome. Previewing a gesture must not spend a turn. A named “Commit turn” action locks the current selection; announce the result in text and retain enough context to understand whose turn follows. Keep the center clear of decorative forms.

## curio-bag

Group a few strange objects by purpose or effect. Selecting one reveals its name and concise effect; inspecting does not consume it. If the game supports use, place a separate “Use item” confirmation beside the target and show the resulting count or state. Do not infer effects or quantities that the game data has not supplied.
