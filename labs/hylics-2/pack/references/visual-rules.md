# Visual rules

## Clay and shape

- Keep sculpted surfaces visibly imperfect: fingerprints, dents, thumb planes, tool marks, joins, and uneven silhouettes.
- Use the generated plate as the source for figure and material cues. CSS may frame or crop it; avoid smooth generic 3D, glossy toy rendering, and flat vector blobs pretending to be clay.
- Let controls bend, tilt, or vary slightly in silhouette. Keep their focus outline and hit area stable so the playfully irregular shape remains usable.
- Treat pixel-like blocks as a secondary graphic accent. Do not turn the full interface into a pixel font or a regular grid.

## Color and hierarchy

Use saturated violet, ultramarine, coral, mint, and acid-lime against a deep blue-violet field. Put bright color behind one clear action at a time. Use a pale label field or strong ink contrast where text sits over the scene.

Party identity, chosen gesture, target, and item effect should be separate readable facts. Use a distinctive selected outline and a short text status, not color alone. Avoid evenly distributing equal-weight panels around every edge.

## Interaction and composition

Keep the central action area visually open. Show who acts, what gesture is previewed, and what a committed turn will affect. Do not make an item look consumed before a separate “Use” confirmation. A party choice or gesture may be changed until its explicit commit.

Use native buttons, keyboard operation, clear focus, adequate hit areas, narrow-screen reflow, and reduced motion. Reserve motion for a selected figure or committed action; do not animate every clay piece continuously.
