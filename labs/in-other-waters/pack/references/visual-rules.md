# Visual rules

## Instrument field

Use warm cream as the working surface, deep blue-green for primary text, clear cyan for topology and scan data, and muted yellow for the active heading or selected specimen. Keep the palette sunlit and legible. Do not turn the interface into a black submarine dashboard or a generic neon-blue HUD.

Use thin contour lines, radial circles, short coordinate ticks, and occasional dot clusters to imply an instrument reading. Let a yellow path or marker sit on top of cyan mapping marks. Pair every unusual symbol with a short text label or visible legend. Keep line density purposeful; preserve quiet areas around controls and labels.

## Navigation and scan language

Make the current location, heading, and selected path easy to find. A radial instrument can show a heading ray and local return marks, while a wider contour plot shows the route and mapped features. Show a simple legend, a coordinate or area label, and an explicit synthetic-reading disclosure.

Scan states need a clear before/after or selected state. Do not imply that a scan completed, a route was traversed, or a specimen was collected unless the local prototype state says so. Use a local button and text status for the demo interaction; never rely only on a color flash.

## Specimens and field records

Specimen entries should combine an original shape mark, sample ID, observation label, and an editable or inspectable note. Keep invented names and measurements visibly within a sample-data context. Field records use time/order labels, location, and one concise observation. Preserve the distinction between observation and interpretation in the note hierarchy.

## Responsive behavior and focus

At desktop widths, a contour plot may sit beside a radial instrument and a compact specimen card. On narrow screens, stack the instrument first, then sample details and field observations. Keep the yellow selected path visible when the map becomes smaller, and do not use horizontal scrolling as the only way to reach controls. Provide visible keyboard focus and use text labels alongside color. Respect reduced motion; no constant rotating sweep is needed.
