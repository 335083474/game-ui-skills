# Visual rules

## Visual grammar

- Build a dense ink-black keyline around color fields. Cut one corner or offset one edge rather than placing every module in a rounded card.
- Use broad lime, cyan, orange, and violet paint swipes as grouping marks. Paint can cross a border, but never run beneath small body text.
- Let labels feel pasted or hand-marked: condensed uppercase, uneven baseline, short number stamps, small halftone patches, and torn-paper edges.
- Keep the largest title and the route direction readable first. Detail copy sits on a solid warm-white or ink backing.
- Use a few exaggerated motion strokes around a route or result; do not animate every element.

## Keep the screens different

Crew selection is about silhouette, name, riding style, and who is active. Use a short horizontal crew rail and one enlarged selected dossier. Do not turn the roster into interchangeable profile tiles.

City missions are about route choice, tag count, time pressure, and a visible path through named districts. Use a drawn route with nodes and one selected stop. Keep route options distinct by objective and risk.

Graffiti results are about how the run was performed. Lead with the score burst and rank stamp, then show separate measures for tag coverage, combo continuity, and route style. Never present these synthetic values as game records.

## Readability and input

- Provide a dark or light solid text plate with at least strong text/background contrast. Keep decorative halftone away from small labels.
- Buttons need a visible selected state and a visible focus ring. Use native buttons so Tab, Enter, and Space work.
- Separate preview from commit. Give the user a clear confirm and cancel path, and state that the data is local demonstration state.
- On narrow screens, stack the crew dossier, route path, and result metrics. Keep route labels readable without horizontal scrolling.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Reserve a short swipe or stamp transition for selection and completion only.

## Boundaries

The package describes an art direction for original interfaces. It does not verify exact game controls, character traits, city names, score formulas, or a complete copy of the game’s interface.
