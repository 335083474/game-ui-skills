# Visual rules

## Architectural wayfinding

- Use open white and cool concrete fields with hard sunlight, quiet gray structural lines, and a single strong signal red.
- Draw red as a path, edge stripe, waypoint marker, or short warning. It should pull the eye toward a route or objective, not decorate every panel.
- Use generous negative space and thin rules. Avoid ornamental borders, large filled cards, gradients, and dense HUD clusters.
- Let architecture do the framing. A roof edge, doorway, or floor line can align with the route cue without becoming a UI panel.
- Use a crisp sans-serif body face and compact uppercase route labels. No downloaded font is required.

## Keep the screens different

Runner Map communicates spatial choice. Use an uncluttered diagram, named route nodes, and a small set of route trade-offs. The path is the strongest visual, not a list of equal cards.

Mission Brief communicates purpose and sequence. Use a stable paper-like placard with a clear target, two or three objectives, a route note, and a single primary start action. This is where longer text belongs.

Movement HUD communicates only the next useful action. Use a small translucent edge cue, one red direction mark, one action label, and a clearly marked demo binding. Hide or dismiss the cue without losing the larger objective. Do not present this specimen as a complete first-person game view.

## Readability and input

- Keep small text on solid white or charcoal backing. Red alone never signals success or failure; pair it with a label and shape.
- Use native buttons, visible focus rings, and separate preview, confirm, and cancel behavior.
- Keep the background city illustrative; do not place essential words over bright sky or detailed texture.
- On mobile, stack the map and route notes, keep the selected line clear, and place movement cues at an edge with room for touch.
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Red route segments can appear instantly when motion is reduced.

## Boundaries

The approved art hypothesis is the original game’s white architecture and red wayfinding. The official web source supports the original title and broad runner/rooftop premise, but it is not a UI manual. This package does not validate exact HUD placement, controls, level geometry, or Catalyst behavior.
