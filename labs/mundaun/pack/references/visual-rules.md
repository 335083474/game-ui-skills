# Visual rules — pencil and paper direction

The official store listing describes a hand-penciled horror story in an Alpine valley. The plan selects this game for a pencil and paper family. These UI rules translate that material into original specimens; they are not captures or reconstructions of the game's screens.

## Material and line

- Use graphite gray on warm gray paper. Build texture from fine hatch, rubbed edges, uneven baselines, and erased gaps instead of smooth gradients or glossy panels.
- Draw object contours with double passes and varied line weight. Add crosshatch to describe volume, cold shadow, or steep terrain.
- Reserve muted oxide red for a route, warning, or one annotation. Keep most of the screen monochrome.
- Use original silhouettes for items and landmarks. Avoid stock icon libraries and polished uniform pictograms.

## Type and hierarchy

- Pair a locally installed book serif for observations with a system monospace for dates, coordinates, and pack counts.
- Keep notes concise and leave physical page margins. A small field caption should not become persistent navigation chrome.
- Preserve enough graphite-to-paper contrast for thin marks to survive small screens.

## Approved patterns

- **satchel:** object drawings have consistent cells but distinct proportions; quantity stays at the edge.
- **journal:** one dated observation pairs with a small landmark sketch.
- **trail-map:** repeated topographic contours, sparse trail marks, and compact orientation metadata.

## Playfield and adaptation

This is a first-person exploration direction. Keep the live HUD to an edge marker or temporary interaction clue. Move the full inventory, journal, and map into explicit pause surfaces so the scene remains available for spatial reading. On mobile, simplify contour density without replacing the pencil language with generic icons.
