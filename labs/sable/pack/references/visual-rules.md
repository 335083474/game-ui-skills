# Visual rules — clear-line desert direction

The plan proposes a Sable family built from thin outlines, flat color planes, and open space. The official store page supports the desert journey, masks, and distinctive art style. The exact visual rules below are our editorial interpretation, not a canonical UI specification.

## Material and line

- Work on sun-warmed paper with a restrained palette of sand, pale sky, rust, muted green, and solar gold.
- Use thin, steady contours around collectibles and landmarks. Favor large flat planes and occasional contour lines over gradients, bevels, shadows, or thick comic outlines.
- Separate regions with a hairline or whitespace rather than stacked cards. Let the empty canvas feel intentional.
- Give each mask a distinct silhouette and a few simple interior marks. Draw original masks; do not trace the game's collectibles.

## Type and hierarchy

- Use a locally installed clean sans-serif for names and headings. A local monospace face suits coordinates and short field labels.
- Put the object or quest name above state, destination, or coordinate detail.
- Make selected and tracked states legible with a rust dot or line and text, not color alone.

## Approved patterns

- **mask-collection:** a small shelf of differentiated masks with found, worn, or rumored state.
- **quest-journal:** a slim index and one active entry with room for a short route clue.
- **desert-map:** dune shapes, one light route trace, and a single destination marker.

## Playfield and adaptation

Keep the landscape as the primary surface while the character moves. Show only a restrained compass, location marker, or temporary prompt; put journal detail and the full map behind deliberate input. On narrow screens, crop the landscape around the active route before adding panel density.
