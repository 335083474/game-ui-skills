# Visual rules

## Natural miniature collage

- Use the generated plate for photographic detail: moss, bark, lichen, wood, fungi, dew, and shallow focus.
- Frame one or two curious objects as if they were found in the terrain. Preserve scale cues; tiny props should sit against broad material detail.
- Build UI from clipped photographs, paper labels, small wood or seed-pod tokens, and quiet shadows. Avoid a bright dashboard panel covering the environment.
- Do not copy the source game's gnome, magic flute, scene composition, or puzzle art. The included traveler object is an original seed-pod and brass construction.

## Pictorial grammar

Prefer a small, consistent family of silhouette symbols and spatial sequences. A path may read left to right; a combination may show two distinct objects meeting; a chapter selector may use a sparse orbit. Labels should be short and secondary.

Every pictorial control still needs a useful accessible name and visible keyboard focus. Include a text equivalent for any clue, selection, or result. Do not rely on color alone to distinguish a route or object state.

## Interaction and composition

Keep object inspection separate from combining. Selecting two objects previews a possible interaction; “Combine” is the explicit attempt. Planet selection likewise previews a route until “Set route” commits it. Make unknown outcomes visibly unknown instead of inventing a puzzle solution.

Use native buttons, clear state feedback, comfortable hit targets, narrow-screen reflow, and reduced motion. Favor a scene-led collage, a central photo, and loose object tokens over a repeated row of equal cards or a dense text inventory.
