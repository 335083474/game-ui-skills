# Visual rules

## Paper construction

- Build depth from real-looking paper planes: visible cut edges, doubled thickness, tabs, and cast shadows.
- Use a few readable mountain or valley folds. Let crease direction explain how a piece unfolds; do not scatter decorative lines without a construction purpose.
- Use the generated plate for fiber, crease, and edge detail. CSS can shape a paper tab or fold shadow, but should not fake the plate's material with noisy filters.
- Keep paper matte. A bright accent may be colored paper, while a small metallic fastener can mark a removable piece.
- Keep this family distinct from felt: no cloth pile, yarn, stitched borders, or wooden button controls.

## Color and type

Start with warm white and pale blue paper, then add small areas of vermilion, leaf green, and butter yellow. Use a dark blue-green ink color for text. A local system sans keeps labels clean; a restrained display serif may be used for a heading, but no proprietary font is bundled.

Preserve contrast across folded edges. A raised or selected paper piece needs a shadow plus outline or text state so its meaning does not depend on color alone.

## Interaction and layout

Show the paper object being collected, worn, or used as a route marker. Keep the component board as a loose maker's table rather than a full-screen mock. Allow selections to move until an explicit pin, unfold, or apply action confirms them.

Use native buttons and radio semantics where appropriate, visible focus, keyboard selection, reduced motion, and a narrow-screen layout that stacks the specimens without clipping their paper edges. Avoid felt motifs, generic tabs floating on a flat dashboard, unexplained creases, and dense text inside tiny cutouts.
