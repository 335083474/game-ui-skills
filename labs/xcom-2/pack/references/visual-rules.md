# Visual rules

## Tactical plate

Use a deep graphite field, muted steel panels, off-white text, a small cold-cyan information cue, and a limited orange commit cue. The frame is square and cut at one or two corners with a thin angled bracket. Do not add bright cyan glow or full-screen scanlines. A subtle single scan band can distinguish a data surface from the playfield, but it never sits under small text.

Keep the mission view visually dominant. During live tactical play, persist only the selected soldier dossier and one compact command group. Put squad inventory, research details, and long status descriptions behind a pause or Avenger surface. Keep the action bar near the screen edge and the center clear for spatial reading.

## Soldier and action language

A dossier is a compact horizontal module: name, class/role, health, armor, status, and two action pips. Use text and small angular insignia; no generated face portraits or initial avatars are needed. A selected soldier uses a thin double bracket and a small named selection line, not a large cyan glow.

Commands show action cost before selection: one action, both actions, or free. Hit chance appears as a clear percentage plus a thin likelihood meter, but the percentage remains the source of truth. A target marker uses the local split-corner reticle SVG. Flanking, cover, and target names each get explicit labels; do not communicate only by red/green.

## Base and research

Equipment is a tight two-column comparison with current and preview values, not a loot-card grid. Research uses a vertical progress track, named scientist or project, and remaining time. Keep the tactical visual grammar present through corner brackets and compressed labels, but reduce the scanline ornament on these text-heavy screens.

## Controls and responsive behavior

Pause or gate any game-camera input while menus are open. Keyboard arrows navigate lists, Enter/Space selects, Escape backs out, and focus uses a bright double rule. On mobile, show one soldier at a time and move secondary controls into a bottom sheet; never stack all squad, target, ability, and objective cards across the playfield. Respect reduced motion and avoid simulated terminal flicker.
